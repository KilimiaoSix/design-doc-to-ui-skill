# 13 Luxury Premium

Sample: `../../generated-samples/13-luxury-premium-style-board.png`

## Style Summary

Luxury premium UI creates a controlled sense of exclusivity through restraint. It uses dark or ivory surfaces, refined spacing, precise typography, high-quality imagery, and subtle metallic accents. The interface should feel quiet, not flashy.

## Visual Language

- Deep black, warm ivory, champagne gold, stone gray, and occasional emerald detail.
- Elegant serif display type with clean sans-serif navigation and controls.
- Large premium photography balanced by sparse cards and hairline dividers.
- Quiet iconography, low component count, and deliberate spacing.
- High contrast surfaces with subtle metallic or paper-grain texture.

## Four-Endpoint Guidance

- Web: use a cinematic but functional booking or concierge layout with clear actions.
- PC: support richer itinerary and membership workflows with side navigation.
- Mobile: keep a single high-value action visible and avoid crowding.
- Tablet: use spacious split layouts for imagery, itinerary, and service cards.

## Prompt Guidance

Anchor the style in a premium service product, such as concierge, travel, private banking, or boutique hospitality. Request real app screens with reservations, itinerary, membership, and support actions.

## Negative Constraints

Avoid excessive gold, perfume-ad layouts, decorative-only imagery, low-contrast gray text, empty luxury moodboards, and brand marks.

## Accessibility Risks

Dark premium palettes can undercut readability. Gold accents often fail contrast on black or ivory. Sparse layouts can hide important operational states if not designed carefully.
